📚 Going deeper — The counter, the rota and the two numbers

The detail, if you want it. Entirely optional — the course works without it.

112 and 166

112 is the European emergency number and it reaches everything from anywhere in Greece. 166 is ΕΚΑΒ, the national ambulance service, if you already know that is what you need. The others, for completeness: 100 police, 199 fire, 108 coastguard, 171 tourist police. The poison centre is a normal Athens landline, 210 7793777, so save it as a contact rather than trying to remember it as a short code.

Καλέστε ασθενοφόρο, παρακαλώ. — Call an ambulance, please.
Πού είναι το νοσοκομείο; — Where is the hospital?

The rota in the window

Pharmacy duty rotas are set by the region on the opinion of the regional pharmacists' association, under a 1991 law. The practical consequence is the one worth carrying: any closed pharmacy has a notice posted in its window naming the ones on duty, with addresses. In Attica the regional association also publishes it at fsa-efimeries.gr. So the dark shutter is not a dead end — it is the directory. Εφημερεύον is the word to look for on the sheet.

Say it before the box crosses the counter

Έχω αλλεργία στην πενικιλίνη — or στα αντιβιοτικά, or στην ασπιρίνη. The noun version is the one to carry, because it comes out the same whoever is saying it; the adjective changes with the speaker (αλλεργικός if you are a man, αλλεργική if you are a woman) and that is one decision too many at a counter. Say it while the pharmacist is still choosing, not after you have paid. And χωρίς is the same word you used with food: έχετε κάτι χωρίς ασπιρίνη, do you have something without aspirin.

Where it hurts, since when

Πονάει εδώ with a hand on the place answers the first question. The second one is almost always από πότε — since when — and a number of days is a complete answer. Έχω πυρετό covers a fever. These three, plus the allergy line, are enough for a pharmacy counter to help you properly.

What the European card actually pays for

With a European health insurance card, admission to a Greek public hospital is free, and so are ΕΣΥ outpatient departments and the Κέντρα Υγείας that are the primary network on the islands. The gaps are specific: you pay 25% of the price of medicine, €1.00 for each electronic prescription and it is not reimbursed, 15% on tests at a contracted private provider, and a share at a contracted private hospital. At a private provider outside the contracted network the card covers nothing. That is the one question worth asking at the door: δημόσιο ή ιδιωτικό — public or private.
