A name that points at two places

Μακεδονία is the name of a region of northern Greece, whose capital is Thessaloniki, and it is also part of the constitutional name of the country next door. That second use is settled in law and in force: the Prespa Agreement was signed on 17 June 2018 at Psarades, approved by the Greek Parliament on 25 January 2019 by 153 votes and in force from 12 February 2019, fixing «Republic of North Macedonia» for all purposes. Behind it sits the Interim Accord of 13 September 1995 and the judgment of the International Court of Justice of 5 December 2011, in case 142, which held that Greece had breached article 11(1) by objecting to the neighbour's NATO admission at Bucharest in 2008 and treated that finding as satisfaction in itself. Those are dates. Whether the agreement was good, fair or legitimate is argued inside both countries, for opposite reasons, and so is any claim about who the name, the history or Alexander belongs to. None of that is your business as a visitor, and none of it needs to be.

Πάω στη Θεσσαλονίκη. — I'm going to Thessaloniki.
Πώς το λέτε εσείς; — How do you say it?

Name the city and the problem stops existing

An English sentence beginning I'm going to Macedonia can land in two opposite ways at the same table, and no amount of goodwill fixes it after the fact. The cheap repair is to be specific: Thessaloniki, Kavala, Ohrid, Skopje. A city name is unambiguous, it is what you actually mean, and it never once reads as a position. Πώς το λέτε εσείς does the same job in reverse — you borrow their word for the place before you use yours.

The sculptures, and the two names for them

What is documented is narrow and worth knowing. Section 3 of the British Museum Act 1963 restricts disposal from the collection to a short list — duplicates, irreparably damaged items, forgeries, objects unnecessary for study — so a definitive return would need new legislation, a position the museum's own de-accession policy of November 2018 restates. In November 2025 the Greek Prime Minister called the talks an impasse, saying they had produced only the offer of a loan, which Greece rejects. At the 25th session of the UNESCO committee on the return of cultural property, twenty countries formally supported the Greek request. Everything else — theft or rescue, what the firman authorised, whether a return sets a precedent — is a position. And the naming matters more than the argument: «Elgin Marbles» and «Parthenon Marbles» both signal a side, and «Parthenon Sculptures» is the descriptive term that does not.

The lighter versions, which run constantly

One city against another, the mainland against the islands, the capital against everywhere else: these run all day and are largely affectionate. They still work the same way. Being asked for a verdict is hospitality, like being passed the bread, and it carries no obligation to answer. Δεν ξέρω αρκετά για να κρίνω and προτιμώ να ακούω are short, they get a nod rather than a groan, and they cost you nothing at all.

The question that always pays

Τι άλλαξε εδώ τα τελευταία χρόνια moves the conversation from the national argument, where you have no standing, to the local one, where the person in front of you is the only available expert. Πού να πάω μετά does something better still: it converts four people mid-argument into four people planning your week. Both are questions, neither is a ruling, and both leave you better informed than the position you did not give.
