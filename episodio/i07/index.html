Name the dish and it becomes carriable

Nobody can walk «everything was lovely» into a kitchen. They can walk «to psari» in. Το ψάρι ήταν εξαιρετικό is six words and it is repeatable by whoever hears it, which is the whole mechanism. Συγχαρητήρια στην κουζίνα is the fixed phrase that addresses the cooking rather than the serving, and it works in a taverna the same way it works at a counter.

Το ψάρι ήταν εξαιρετικό. — The fish was outstanding.
Συγχαρητήρια στην κουζίνα! — Compliments to the kitchen!

Signomi plus the fact

Αυτό είναι κρύο. Δεν παρήγγειλα αυτό. Θα αργήσει πολύ; Three sentences, no adjectives about how the evening has gone, no account of your day. The shorter it is, the faster the plate moves — and the opener does the softening so the content does not have to.

Συγγνώμη, αυτό είναι κρύο. — Excuse me, this is cold.
Μπορείτε να το αλλάξετε, παρακαλώ; — Could you change it, please?

Two politeness systems, pointed in different directions

Descriptive work on Greek and English politeness places them on opposite settings: Greek oriented to involvement and closeness, English to distance and non-imposition. That is why the same flat factual sentence you would hedge three times at home lands here as ordinary information, and why a warmly delivered Greek exchange can read to you as more forward than it is meant to be. The mismatch runs both ways and neither side is being rude.

Why the over-apology backfires

Stacking sorries on top of a fact buries the one piece of information the other person needs and signals that you are braced for an argument. Signomi once, the fact, then silence. If the fix comes, ευχαριστώ πολύ closes it; if it does not, you have still said the thing out loud rather than typed it into a phone in the taxi.

The strongest compliment is the return

Θα ξανάρθουμε σίγουρα outranks any adjective available to you. A room that feeds the same neighbourhood every week counts its work in the people who walk back in, and saying it on the way out costs four words.
