The detail, if you want it. Entirely optional — the course works without it.

There is no cover charge here

Article 72 of the Greek market conduct rules bans κουβέρ outright, and goes further: no product may be served and then charged without prior consent, with bread and bottled water named explicitly. The penalty is five hundred euros per item, and it has been issued in real inspections. Bread is still allowed on a bill — but only where it was offered, priced and accepted. If it landed silently and appears as a line, the sequence was wrong, and one flat sentence is all it takes to say so.

Το νερό δεν το παραγγείλαμε. — We didn't order the water.
Το ψωμί δεν το ζητήσαμε. — We didn't ask for the bread.

Why this surprises anyone who has been to Italy

The Italian coperto is lawful when it is declared: you agree to it by sitting down at a place that has written it on the menu. Greece runs the opposite logic. There is no lawful flat charge for the privilege of the table, and the bread and water that arrive with it are goods like any other goods — they need an offer and a yes before they need a price. Travellers who have learned to shrug at the coperto often pay a Greek line they did not have to.

The list you can read from the pavement

Articles 70 and 71 make a price list compulsory: written in Greek, displayed visibly at the entrance and again before you order, with prices including tax. No list is a thousand euro penalty on the business; a price charged above the listed one is five hundred per item. The practical value of this is that it happens before you commit. You can stand on the pavement, read the board, and choose — which is a much better place to have the conversation than a table with your coat already on the chair.

Whose job the receipt is

Issuing the printed receipt is the business's legal obligation under Law 4308/2014, with issue through a certified fiscal device required since the start of 2015. So asking for it is not an accusation and not a favour; it is asking someone to do a thing they were going to have to do anyway. What you should not believe is the widely repeated claim that a customer can be penalised for walking out without one — no such provision has been located.

Tipping: the mechanics, and the number nobody has

Tips exist and are recognised — a 2024 law exempts up to three hundred euros a month per worker from income tax and the whole amount from social contributions, which is about as institutional as recognition gets. What does not exist is a defensible percentage. No survey, sector federation or statistical source produces one. So learn the mechanics — ask for the bill, round the coins up or leave a small note — and treat any figure quoted online as somebody's guess rather than a rule you are breaking.
