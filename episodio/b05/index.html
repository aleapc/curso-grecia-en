True beats fluent

Never repeat this part's ten days when your own ticket says something else. Swap the number for the truth; the sentence stays the same length and the same shape, and δέκα is the only word in it that changes.

One question, one payload

Purpose, length and address are three separate answers, not one speech. Stacking all three before you were asked for them is the most common way a thirty-second exchange turns into a three-minute one — and it is the habit visitors bring with them, not something the desk provokes.

Πόσες μέρες θα μείνετε; — How many days will you stay?
Δέκα μέρες. — Ten days.

Ορίστε, and the paper doing the talking

Ορίστε is one of the most useful words in the language and it has three jobs: here you are, as you hand something over; go ahead, when someone approaches you; and sorry, what?, said with a rising tone. At a desk it is the first of the three, and it moves the exchange off your memory and onto your evidence.

The word that changes with who is speaking

Μόνος μου and μόνη μου are the same sentence said by a man and by a woman. Greek does this in a lot of places, and here it is a single letter at the end. Learn the one that is yours and never think about the other again.

Ταξιδεύω μόνος μου. — I'm travelling alone. (a man speaking)
Ταξιδεύω μόνη μου. — I'm travelling alone. (a woman speaking)

The rules move, so check them close to departure

This part teaches the conversation at the desk, not the paperwork behind it. Entry requirements change, and a course file is the wrong place to read them from — check the official source for your own passport shortly before you fly.
