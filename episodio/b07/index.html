The detail, if you want it. Entirely optional — the course works without it.

Μπορώ να plus the verb

Keep Μπορώ να and put after it whatever you want to do. The να is what holds the verb in a single shape, so nothing in front of it ever moves — which is exactly why this is the cheapest frame in the course for a language that otherwise changes the ends of its words. The question mark you hear is a rise in the voice; the one you write is a semicolon.

Μπορώ να πληρώσω με κάρτα; — Can I pay by card?
Μπορώ να αφήσω τη βαλίτσα εδώ; — Can I leave the suitcase here?

Three ways in from the airport, three prices

The metro runs from the airport for nine euros one way and sixteen return. The Express bus is five fifty and runs when the metro does not. The taxi rank works on a posted flat fare into the ring road, day rate and night rate, tolls and tax inside the number. Choosing is a matter of luggage and hour, not of nerve.

Card is an obligation, not a favour

Greek law obliges traders in a long list of activity codes to accept card payment, and obliges them to say so visibly at the entrance and at the till. The list was extended in 2023 to a further thirty-five codes, reaching open-air markets, food retail and kiosks. That is a stronger position than the traveller has in Italy or France — but it is a list, not the whole of commerce, so the useful move is still to ask before you commit.

The receipt belongs to them

Issuing the receipt is the trader's legal duty, not something you have to argue for. Asking for one costs a single frame — Μπορώ να πάρω απόδειξη; — and it is a completely ordinary thing to say, not a challenge.

The verbs worth carrying

πληρώσω pay · αγοράσω buy · καθίσω sit · περάσω get past · κατέβω get off · αφήσω leave · πάρω take · δοκιμάσω try. Eight verbs behind one opening covers most of what a first day needs to ask.
