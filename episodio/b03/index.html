The open frame

Keep συγγνώμη and change only what comes after it. Nothing in the opener bends: you never conjugate it, never adapt it to who you are talking to, and it works standing, sitting, in a queue or through a window. It is the cheapest thing you own and the one you will use most.

Συγγνώμη, δεν κατάλαβα. — Sorry, I didn't catch that.
Συγγνώμη, περνάω; — Sorry, can I get through?

The word itself, one more time

Double gamma before nu is ng plus n — the joint in the middle of wing nut. Build it as sing–no–me and then close the gaps, with the beat on the middle piece. Under pressure it will come out approximately, and approximately is enough: the word is long, distinctive and unmistakable even when the middle is soft.

Συγγνώμη. — seeng-NO-mee — sorry; excuse me

Ask for the version you can use

There are three different requests hiding inside 'I didn't get that', and they produce three different results. Άλλη μια φορά gets the same sentence again. Πιο αργά gets it stretched out. Μπορείτε να το γράψετε gets it on paper, which is the only one that survives a platform number, a price or a street name. Choose by what you are missing, not by what feels politest.

The half-second that makes it work

Say the opener, then stop. The pause is what turns your sentence into a request rather than an interruption, and it gives the other person time to switch out of whatever they were doing. Running the opener straight into the question throws away most of what the word is doing for you.

Repair comes third on purpose

Every later part of this course assumes you will mishear something. The cost of mishearing drops to almost nothing the moment you own one opener that buys a second attempt, so it arrives before the vocabulary rather than after it. It is also the frame that comes back in the parts on directions, on signs and on getting served — the same three syllables, a different ending each time.
