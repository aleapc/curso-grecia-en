Flat delivery, inverted meaning

Σιγά on its own, and the fuller σιγά τα λάχανα — literally slow the cabbages — mean big deal, and they are said without any change of pace to mark the joke. Καλά, which you learned as fine, does the same trick: level and slightly slow, it is polite disbelief rather than agreement. Because nothing in the delivery flags it, this register is invisible to an ear still working on vocabulary, which is exactly why it sits in this module rather than in the basic one.

Σιγά τα λάχανα! — Big deal!
Καλά, σοβαρά τώρα; — Right, seriously?

Πλάκα and δουλειά

Πλάκα κάνω is the standard closer on a tease: I am joking. It comes fast, often before the tease has finished landing, and its speed is the point — it keeps the sharpness inside the friendship. Σε δουλεύω is the same move with a different image: δουλεύω means to work, and to work someone is to wind them up. Turned round, it is what you say when you have just caught one, and saying it is itself a way of joining in. Keep your own version in the -ετε form — με δουλεύετε — the way every other sentence you produce in this course is built: the εσύ/εσείς choice is a signal you read in other people, never a decision you make under pressure, and A07 comes back to exactly that.

The genres that are not yours

Jokes about a neighbouring country, jokes about one Greek city against another, jokes about a region and its people all run constantly and all of them are internal. A visitor repeating one is not participating in a shared game; it reads as an outsider passing judgement on a family argument, and it costs more goodwill than the laugh is worth. Two topics deserve the same treatment for the same reason: religion, and any comparison between parts of the country. Listening is free. Retelling is not, and neither is having a view.

Εγώ δεν λέω τίποτα

Learn this one by heart. It means I am saying nothing, it is delivered with a smile and both hands up, and it is the cheapest exit available from any conversation where somebody wants a foreigner to settle an argument about their own country. It gets a laugh because everybody at the table recognises the move. Nothing else you could say buys as much for as little.

The joke that is reliably yours

Make yourself the subject. Your Greek, the amount you have eaten, the pudding you went back for, and above all the alphabet: reading ΡΟΔΟΣ aloud as Podos because the rho looks like a p is the visitor's error, and telling it against yourself is funny precisely because everyone has watched somebody do it. Self-deprecation is the one register that travels intact, and it also does something useful — it gets the anxiety about your accent out of the room before anybody has to be polite about it.
