Why Easter moves away from yours

Orthodox Easter is worked out on the Julian calendar. The equinox is fixed at 21 March Julian, which is 3 April in the calendar on your phone; then the full moon; then the following Sunday; plus the rule that it may not fall before or together with Passover. The two calendars are thirteen days apart at present, which is why the dates usually sit weeks apart rather than on top of each other. They did coincide in 2025, on 20 April, and they meet again in 2028, on 16 April. Everything about your week — ferries, opening hours, whether a village has three hundred extra people in it — hangs on which of the two you are standing inside.

The nine that are fixed by name

Article 60 of law 4808/2021 lists the compulsory public holidays: 1 January, 6 January, 25 March, Easter Monday, 1 May, 15 August, 28 October, 25 December and 26 December. Nine dates, and Easter Monday is the moving one. The surprise for most visitors is what is not there: Good Friday. It is a holiday for the public sector and for schools, and in the private sector it follows sector rules rather than the compulsory list — which is why some shutters are down and others are not on a day that feels, from the street, like the most solemn of the year.

July and January are two different countries

Greece took 29.3 million international air arrivals in 2024. July alone was 5.0 million of them, 16.9 percent of the year; January was 648 thousand, 2.2 percent. That is a ratio of roughly eight to one between the same two islands in two different months. The useful part is what has been moving: the June-to-August share fell from 56 percent in 2019 to 50 percent in 2024, with spring up 21 percent and winter up 16 percent. The season is widening at both ends, which is exactly where the good weeks are. What nobody can tell you from a source is which specific island closes on which date — that varies radically between a large resort island and a small one, so the question belongs to the door, not to the statistics.

Sunday, and why it never applied to you

Sunday closing for shops goes back to 1909, and from the very beginning it exempted restaurants, tavernas, patisseries, cafés and shops selling tourist goods. In other words, the side of the economy a visitor actually uses was never inside the rule. The present regime is article 16 of law 4177/2013, with declared exceptions and tourist zones set by ministerial decision. It is worth knowing that this is live, contested ground — one liberalisation decision was struck down as unconstitutional — so treat any general claim about Sunday trading as unstable, and ask the specific shop.

The one question that beats every list

Είστε ανοιχτά αύριο, asked the evening before, is worth more than any holiday table you could memorise, because it resolves the one door you need rather than the country. Add πότε ανοίγετε πάλι when the answer is no, and τι ώρα κλείνετε σήμερα when the answer is yes but the afternoon is running out. Three sentences, and you never plan a day around a guess.
